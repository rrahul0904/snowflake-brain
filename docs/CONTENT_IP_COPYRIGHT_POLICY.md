# Content Intellectual Property & Copyright Policy

**Status:** Launch policy  
**Applies to:** study guides, practice questions, mock exams, explanations, articles, UI copy, graphics, source code, datasets, generated content, contributor submissions, and any material published by or distributed through this project.

> This policy is an operational content standard, not a substitute for advice from qualified intellectual-property counsel.

## 1. Core rule

The platform publishes **independently authored certification-preparation material**. We do not knowingly copy, reproduce, distribute, solicit, buy, import, or reconstruct confidential certification questions, exam dumps, braindumps, recalled live exam items, or unlicensed third-party commercial training content.

Facts, product behavior, public certification objectives, and ideas may be researched from lawful public sources. The platform's wording, question construction, explanations, examples, diagrams, organization, and instructional expression must be original or used under a documented license.

## 2. Permitted source material

Content authors may use:

- official public Snowflake documentation;
- public SnowPro certification pages, exam objectives, and publicly released study guidance;
- public standards, specifications, and factual technical references;
- material created by the platform operator or contributors who have granted the necessary rights;
- open-source or Creative Commons material only when its license permits the intended use and all attribution/share-alike requirements are satisfied;
- short quotations only when legally appropriate, necessary, accurately attributed, and approved during editorial review.

A public source may be used to verify facts. It must **not** be treated as permission to copy the source's protected expression.

## 3. Prohibited source material

The following material must not enter the authoring, retrieval, prompt, training, fine-tuning, question-generation, or publication pipeline unless documented written permission clearly authorizes the intended use:

- live, leaked, stolen, recalled, reconstructed, or confidential certification exam questions or answer keys;
- braindumps, exam dumps, "actual exam" repositories, Telegram/Discord dump collections, or similar sources;
- paid or restricted practice-test wording, explanations, screenshots, videos, PDFs, books, or course materials from third parties;
- scraped or copied website articles where the license does not permit republication;
- proprietary diagrams, screenshots, logos, icons, photos, or artwork without sufficient rights;
- content supplied by a contributor who cannot establish the right to provide it;
- prompts containing substantial unlicensed third-party content for the purpose of generating a substitute or derivative version.

If provenance is unclear, the material is quarantined and is not published.

## 4. Certification-question integrity rules

Every production question must be independently written and must satisfy all of the following:

1. It maps to a public certification objective or a documented technical learning objective.
2. Its stem, answer choices, rationale, distractor logic, and examples are independently authored.
3. It does not claim to be a real, leaked, recalled, or previously administered SnowPro exam item.
4. It does not intentionally imitate distinctive wording from a commercial question bank.
5. Technical facts are traceable to lawful sources, preferably official documentation.
6. Source references are stored as provenance metadata where the question-bank format permits it.
7. Material receives structural QA and editorial/SME review before unrestricted publication.
8. Any credible similarity or ownership complaint triggers immediate review and, where appropriate, temporary removal.

The private COF-C03 bank manifest is the current baseline: it records that the corpus is independently authored and that official `docs.snowflake.com` references are attached to questions for factual verification.

## 5. AI-assisted authoring

AI may assist with drafting, variation, classification, QA, or editing, but it does not eliminate provenance obligations.

AI-assisted material must:

- start from lawful inputs;
- not be prompted to reproduce a paid course, book, exam dump, or protected question bank;
- be reviewed for factual accuracy, originality, and accidental close copying;
- be treated as draft content until a human editorial gate is satisfied;
- preserve relevant provenance and review records.

When practical, high-value corpora should be checked for suspicious phrase overlap against known internal content and any third-party material the operator is legally entitled to compare against.

## 6. Trademarks and certification names

Snowflake, SnowPro, and related Snowflake names and marks belong to Snowflake Inc. or their respective owners. They may be referenced only as needed to identify the technology or certification being discussed.

The platform must:

- clearly state that it is independent and is not affiliated with, sponsored by, approved by, or endorsed by Snowflake Inc.;
- avoid using Snowflake logos or other brand artwork without permission;
- avoid presenting Snowflake or SnowPro as the platform's own brand;
- use a distinct platform brand for trademark registration and commercial identity;
- avoid titles, logos, domains, or visual treatments that are likely to create confusion about source or sponsorship.

## 7. Copyright ownership and contributor rights

Original platform-authored content is intended to be owned by the platform operator or the applicable legal entity, subject to any contributor or employment agreements.

Before accepting material from employees, contractors, SMEs, guest authors, or other contributors, obtain a written agreement that addresses at least:

- authorship and ownership;
- assignment or license of copyright and other necessary rights;
- permission to edit, publish, commercialize, and create adaptations;
- a warranty that the contributor is not providing confidential exam content or infringing third-party material;
- disclosure of third-party/open-source components and their licenses.

Do not assume payment alone transfers copyright.

## 8. Public copyright notice

Published pages should display a copyright notice for the platform operator's original material and link to the public Copyright & IP Notice.

Before commercial launch, replace generic operator references with the exact individual or legal entity that owns the content.

## 9. Copyright complaints and takedowns

A credible infringement complaint must be logged, acknowledged, and reviewed promptly. The operator may temporarily disable disputed material while investigating.

The review record should capture:

- complainant and contact information;
- the copyrighted work claimed to be infringed;
- the platform URL/item identifier at issue;
- the source/provenance record for the disputed item;
- similarity findings;
- removal, replacement, restoration, or licensing decision;
- date and reviewer.

If the service later hosts user-uploaded/user-posted material and seeks U.S. DMCA safe-harbor protection, the operator must separately evaluate DMCA requirements, publish the required designated-agent contact information, and register the agent with the U.S. Copyright Office.

## 10. Release gate

No content release is "IP cleared" merely because a disclaimer exists. A release passes this gate only when:

- provenance is known;
- the use is original, licensed, public-domain, or otherwise legally authorized;
- required attribution/license notices are present;
- no confidential-exam or dump source was used;
- trademark/non-affiliation language is accurate;
- high-risk complaints are resolved;
- ownership of commissioned/contributed material is documented.

## 11. Record retention

Maintain versioned evidence of authorship and provenance, including Git history, source-reference metadata, corpus hashes, editorial review records, contributor agreements, licenses, and registration certificates. Do not expose private question-bank content merely to prove provenance.
